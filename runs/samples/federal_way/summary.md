(SECTION 3 - SCOPE OF WORK AND TIMELINE, p. 5 [S5.1]; Scope, p. 5 [S6.1])

## Proposed solution

The bid proposes a single integrated ERP suite for the City of Federal Way — Financials (General Ledger, Budget, Project and Grant Accounting, Purchasing and Contract Management, Accounts Payable, Accounts Receivable, Capital Assets, Enterprise Cashiering) plus Human Capital Management (Human Resources, Time and Attendance, Payroll) — delivered as a configured, hosted-or-on-premises commercial product with implementation services, not a custom build. Delivery runs in phases from March 2025 to a full-suite Go Live before January 1, 2027: mobilise and process design, then Financials, then HCM and integrations, then stabilisation and post-go-live support. The team is a named vendor implementation group (project manager, functional leads per module, data conversion and integration specialists, report developers, trainers and change managers) working alongside City subject-matter experts whose time commitments are stated by role. Pricing is submitted on Exhibit A with a separate sheet per deployment option, itemised by module, service line and Years 2–10 subscription, and held firm for 180 days. A governance and compliance component carries the contractual, insurance, ownership and disclosure obligations — including the bid-phase question deadline — so they are not lost among the technical work.

### K01. Requirements document not in hand, so scope and price rest on an unseen document

The functional scope the bid must price and configure is defined in a separate ERP Requirements document that is not part of the RFP text available to the team. If the document contains line items beyond the module list summarised in the RFP, the bid either under-prices the work or must rate items it has not costed, and the Software criterion (30 points) is scored directly on that document. The bidder carries the cost of any rework to the Requirements ratings and Exhibit A after the document is received.

**Why:** The scope baseline for both the Requirements response and the Exhibit A price is a document the team does not yet hold, and the RFP makes blank or unrated lines grounds for rejection as incomplete. (SECTION 3 - SCOPE OF WORK AND TIMELINE, p. 5 [S5.1]; SECTION 5 – VENDOR INSTRUCTIONS, p. 8 [S9.1]; SECTION 5 – VENDOR INSTRUCTIONS, p. 9 [S9.3]; SECTION 6 – REQUIREMENTS, p. 11 [S10.1])

**Mitigation:** Obtain the Requirements document and Exhibit A before any pricing work starts; state in the proposal that the price is based on that document as issued and that line items not in it are out of scope and handled by change order.

**Related requirements:** R005, R011, R017, R039, R040, R093.

### K02. Go Live date treated as firm when the RFP only scores it

The proposed plan commits to all modules live before January 1, 2027 and builds the schedule backwards from that date, but the RFP states the date as an evaluation criterion rather than a contractual milestone. If the City later treats it as a hard date, or if fit-gap reveals more configuration than expected, the bidder absorbs the cost of acceleration or carries the risk of a missed date on a fixed price. Conversely, if the City would have accepted a later date, the bid may have priced unnecessary contingency.

**Why:** The RFP does not settle whether a later Go Live is rejected or merely scored lower, and the solution has chosen to treat the date as binding without a stated assumption to that effect in the commercial terms. (Timeline, p. 6 [S7.1]; SECTION 4 – RFP EVALUATION, p. 7 [S8.1])

**Mitigation:** Ask the City in writing before August 28, 2024 whether a later Go Live is disqualifying or only scored lower, and state in the proposal that the January 1, 2027 date assumes timely City decisions, SME availability and no scope growth beyond the Requirements document.

**Related requirements:** R016, R018, R012.

### K03. Proposal format and section order not fully specified, risking non-responsiveness

The RFP refers to a required order of proposal contents and a list of required sections that are not present in the text available to the team, and it warns that deviations may be classified as non-responsive. The solution plans a 100-page PDF plus a Word Requirements document but cannot confirm the section sequence. A mis-ordered or over-length submission could be disqualified before it is scored.

**Why:** The consequence of a non-responsive finding is total loss of the bid, but the solution's structure follows the RFP's stated components and the risk turns on a table the team has not yet seen. (SECTION 5 – VENDOR INSTRUCTIONS, p. 8 [S9.1]; SECTION 5 – VENDOR INSTRUCTIONS, pp. 8-9 [S9.2]; SECTION 5 – VENDOR INSTRUCTIONS, p. 9 [S9.3])

**Mitigation:** Obtain the submission table and required section list from the City, build the PDF to that exact order, and run a page-count and format check against the 100-page limit before the September 17, 2024 deadline.

**Related requirements:** R030, R035, R041, R031.

### K04. Timeline can be rescheduled by the City after the price is fixed

The RFP reserves the City's right to modify or reschedule the timeline, while the proposed solution assumes the March 2025 start and published dates hold and that any rescheduling is handled by change order. If the City moves the start or stretches the phases, vendor resources committed for the original window may not be available and the fixed price may no longer cover the delivery. The bidder either absorbs the difference or disputes it after award.

**Why:** The RFP explicitly reserves the right to reschedule, and the solution's assumption that rescheduling is a change order is the bidder's reading, not something the RFP confirms. (Timeline, p. 6 [S7.1])

**Mitigation:** State in the proposal that pricing is based on a March 2025 start and the published phase durations, and that a start delayed beyond an agreed threshold triggers a schedule and price adjustment.

**Related requirements:** R016, R001.

### K05. Both deployment options priced without knowing which are required

The solution prices a SaaS/hosted option and an on-premises option on separate Exhibit A sheets, but the RFP does not say whether both must be priced or only the proposed one. Pricing both doubles the commercial and technical effort and risks the City comparing the two in a way that disadvantages the bid, while pricing only one risks a non-responsive submission. The bidder also carries the cost of documenting two architectures, two security models and two sets of hardware specifications.

**Why:** The RFP's pricing template says one sheet per deployment option 'if applicable' without defining applicability, and the solution has resolved the ambiguity by pricing both. (SECTION 5 – VENDOR INSTRUCTIONS, p. 9 [S9.3]; Exhibit A – Pricing, p. 14 [S12.1]; SECTION 5 – VENDOR INSTRUCTIONS, p. 10 [S9.4])

**Mitigation:** Ask the City before the question deadline whether both SaaS and on-premises sheets are required, and if unanswered, submit both with a clear statement of which option the bidder recommends and why.

**Related requirements:** R047, R114, R066, R067, R069.

### K06. Insurance priced without stated coverage types or limits

The RFP requires a certificate of insurance and an underwriter's endorsement naming the City as additional insured but states no coverage types, limits or additional insured parties. The solution assumes commercial general liability, professional liability, cyber liability and workers' compensation. If the City's contract requires higher limits or coverages the bidder does not carry, the bidder faces unplanned premium cost or an inability to commence work, which delays the March 2025 start.

**Why:** The RFP leaves the coverage schedule open, and the solution's assumption of four coverage lines is an inference rather than a confirmed requirement. (SECTION 7 – TERMS AND CONDITIONS, p. 13 [S11.3])

**Mitigation:** Ask the City for the required coverage types, limits and additional insured wording before submission, and confirm with the broker that the assumed coverages can be endorsed with a Washington-admitted surety before any work commences.

**Related requirements:** R111, R112.

### K07. Best-of-breed HCM evaluation path unclear, affecting teaming and price

The RFP invites best-of-breed HCM vendors but does not say whether such a submission is evaluated standalone or only alongside an ERP vendor. The solution offers a single integrated suite with best-of-breed HCM only as an alternative carrying a demonstrated integration. If the City expected a standalone HCM award or a partnered submission, the bid may be scored against a structure it did not address, and the integration effort priced for the alternative may be misjudged.

**Why:** The RFP does not settle how best-of-breed submissions are evaluated, and the solution has chosen a suite-first structure without confirming that choice with the City. (SECTION 1 - REQUEST FOR PROPOSALS (RFP), p. 3 [S3.1])

**Mitigation:** Ask the City how best-of-breed HCM submissions are evaluated and whether a partnered ERP-plus-HCM response is acceptable, and state in the proposal which structure is being submitted and on what basis it is priced.

**Related requirements:** R007, R011, R017.

### K08. Demo script not issued, so demo preparation is scoped blind

Finalists must demonstrate against a pre-defined Demo Script that is issued only with the finalist notification, yet the solution prices demo preparation as a fixed effort in the implementation services line. If the script covers scenarios outside the configured demonstration environment or requires modules not yet configured, the team must build additional demo content at short notice. The demo is scored on adherence to the script, so a poor fit costs evaluation points as well as effort.

**Why:** The script is a referenced document not included in the RFP, and the solution's fixed demo effort is an assumption about its size and content. (SECTION 4 – RFP EVALUATION, p. 7 [S8.1, S8.2])

**Mitigation:** State in the proposal that demo preparation is priced on a script of comparable scope to the requirements set, and reserve the right to re-scope demo effort once the script is issued; keep a rehearsed generic scenario library ready.

**Related requirements:** R020, R021, R022.

### K09. Legacy systems and data volumes unknown, so conversion is under-scoped

The RFP's tables describing the current environment and the systems in scope are not available, and the solution assumes a set of legacy systems converted module by module with history depth agreed during fit-gap. If the legacy estate is larger, dirtier or more numerous than assumed, data conversion and reconciliation effort grows after the fixed price is set. Conversion is a separately itemised and priced line, so the overrun lands directly on the bidder.

**Why:** The specific systems and data volumes are not given in the RFP, and the solution's conversion scope rests on an assumption rather than on the City's inventory. (SECTION 3 - SCOPE OF WORK AND TIMELINE, p. 5 [S5.1]; Scope, p. 5 [S6.1])

**Mitigation:** Ask the City for the current environment tables and data volumes before submission, and state in the proposal the assumed number of source systems, history years and cleansing rules, with additional conversions priced as change orders.

**Related requirements:** R055, R048, R118.

### K10. Unreadable page may hide submission or pricing instructions

Page 5 of the RFP is dominated by an image block that could not be read, and it may carry instructions on submission, pricing or scope that the bid does not reflect. If it contains a mandatory instruction, the proposal could be judged non-responsive or priced on a wrong basis. The bidder would only discover this after the deadline.

**Why:** A page covering 42% of its area with an unparsed image is a real blind spot, and the RFP's submission and pricing rules are strict enough that a missed instruction is costly.

**Mitigation:** Obtain a readable copy of page 5 from the City or the issuing contact before the question deadline and confirm in writing that no instruction on that page has been missed.

### K11. 100-word limit applied broadly may crowd out required narrative

The solution applies the 100-word limit to every per-requirement comment and, for safety, to narrative responses as well, because the RFP does not say which responses the limit covers. If the limit does not apply to the Implementation and Support narratives, the bid may under-describe methodology, resourcing and support levels that carry 25 and 15 scoring points respectively. If it does apply and the bid ignores it, the submission risks being marked non-compliant.

**Why:** The RFP's wording is genuinely unclear on the scope of the limit, and the solution has chosen the conservative reading, which trades evaluation strength for compliance safety. (SECTION 5 – VENDOR INSTRUCTIONS, p. 9 [S9.3]; SECTION 6 – REQUIREMENTS, p. 11 [S10.2])

**Mitigation:** Ask the City whether the 100-word limit applies to narrative sections, and if unanswered, keep per-requirement comments within the limit while writing full narrative sections and noting the interpretation in the cover letter.

**Related requirements:** R043, R040, R087.

### K12. Reference qualification criteria undefined, risking a weak Vendor score

The RFP requires three current and two prior customer references of similar size and scope, with preference for Washington State public sector clients, but does not define 'similar size', 'current' or whether references must run the same product and modules. The solution assumes references from customers running the same product and modules with Washington public-sector clients preferred. If the City applies a stricter test, the bid may lose Vendor points or be found non-responsive on references.

**Why:** References carry 15 Vendor points and the criteria are undefined, but the solution's reference selection already targets the preferred profile, so the exposure is to scoring rather than to delivery. (SECTION 5 – VENDOR INSTRUCTIONS, p. 10 [S9.4]; Exhibit B - References Current Customers, p. 15 [S13.1]; Prior Customers, p. 16 [S15.1])

**Mitigation:** Ask the City what qualifies a reference as similar in size and scope and whether product and module match is required, and obtain each reference's consent to be contacted before submission.

**Related requirements:** R079, R080, R081, R082.
