## Problem statement

### Client situation

- The RFP is issued by Empire State Development (ESD), a New York State economic development corporation whose mission is to promote a growing state economy, encourage business investment and job creation, and support diverse local economies through loans, grants, tax credits, real estate development and marketing. (Issued: August 8, 2025, p. 1 [S2.1]; I. INTRODUCTION, p. 3 [S5.1])
- ESD's Office of Strategic Workforce Development (OSWD) was created as part of a $350 million workforce development investment announced by Governor Kathy Hochul in April 2022, and is charged with aligning workforce programs to employer needs and creating opportunities for unemployed, underemployed and underrepresented workers. (II. OVERVIEW, p. 3 [S6.1])
- ESD has identified Artificial Intelligence as one of the state's highest-growth sectors, pointing to Cornell Tech research, companies using AI for analytics and automation, and state-backed initiatives such as Empire AI. (II. OVERVIEW, p. 3 [S6.1])
- ESD's stated pain point is that rapid AI advancement risks widening economic inequality, because many well-paying AI jobs require specialized education and technical training that lower-income New Yorkers cannot access. (II. OVERVIEW, p. 3 [S6.1])
- ESD also cites tech recruiting practices that favor students at elite schools and those with personal connections to prominent technology companies as a barrier AI Prep is meant to level. (Artificial Intelligence Prep – "AI Prep", pp. 3-4 [S7.1])
- The procurement is subject to a Procurement Lobbying Law restricted period running until contract approval, during which bidders may contact only the designated contacts (Julia Borukhov, primary; Jordan Kaplan, secondary) by email at AI.Prep@esd.ny.gov. (Issued: August 8, 2025, p. 1 [S2.1]; iii. State Finance Law Sections 139-j and 139-k forms, p. 16 [S39.1])

### Goal

- ESD seeks to award contracts to up to two qualified program administrators to develop the curriculum for and administer the AI Prep applied training program, covering Track A and/or Track B. (Artificial Intelligence Prep – "AI Prep", pp. 3-4 [S7.1]; Program Goals and Objectives, pp. 5-6 [S12.1])
- Track A, "AI Prep 4 Internships," requires the administrator to deliver a comprehensive AI technology training program for low-income matriculating undergraduates, including paid internship placements at leading New York tech companies leading ultimately to full-time jobs. (Artificial Intelligence Prep – "AI Prep", pp. 3-4 [S7.1]; For Track A: AI Prep 4 Internships, p. 4 [S8.1])
- Track B, "AI Prep 4 Jobs," requires an accelerated training or bootcamp alternative to degree programs that equips low-income New Yorkers with barriers to employment with in-demand AI technical and professional skills and connects them to full-time AI jobs. (Artificial Intelligence Prep – "AI Prep", pp. 3-4 [S7.1]; For Track B: AI Prep 4 Jobs, p. 5 [S9.1])
- Both tracks must include targeted outreach and recruitment, an application and assessment process, industry-informed online AI curriculum, participant stipends and wrap-around supports such as mentors or success coaches, and employer relationship-building to generate placements. (For Track A: AI Prep 4 Internships, p. 4 [S8.1]; For Track B: AI Prep 4 Jobs, p. 5 [S9.1])
- The selected administrator(s) will operate the program over an initial term of up to 18 months, with possible renewal or extension based on performance, need and availability of funds, and an anticipated contract start date of January 1, 2026. (For Track B: AI Prep 4 Jobs, p. 5 [S9.1]; Grant Funding and Project Timeline, p. 6 [S14.1]; V. SCHEDULE OF DATES, p. 7 [S18.1])

## Proposed solution

ESD is buying program administration, not software: the bidder delivers a full-service AI Prep operating capability — outreach, assessment, industry-informed online curriculum, stipends and wrap-around supports, employer placement, and outcome reporting — configured around ESD's two tracks and priced within the $2M-per-track cap. The solution is built in four phases: a 60-day mobilisation after the anticipated 1 January 2026 start (curriculum finalisation, employer commitments, systems and compliance set-up), a launch cohort recruited and assessed against a defined low-income standard, delivery of immersive part-time or full-time online training with mentors and career coaches, and placement plus outcome tracking through the 18-month initial term. The bidder proposes as a single lead entity (with named subcontractors disclosed in the proposal) and offers Track A, Track B, or both, with a separate costed budget for each track so ESD can award one or two administrators. Delivery is by a named core team of program director, curriculum lead, employer partnerships lead, success coaches and a data/compliance analyst, all subject to ESD approval and continuity expectations. A dedicated compliance workstream runs alongside delivery to carry the MWBE/SDVOB, insurance, lobbying-law, vendor-responsibility and reporting obligations that the contract imposes, and it also owns the bid-side calendar that gets the bidder's questions to ESD by the August 29, 2025 deadline.

### Coverage

Covers 109 of 109 mandatory requirements.

### Components

#### C01. Program design, curriculum and credential architecture

Delivers the industry-informed online AI curriculum for Track A and Track B, built with an employer advisory group that reviews technical skills and learning milestones each cohort. The curriculum is structured as an immersive schedule that can run part-time or full-time, with defined skill-attainment assessments at each milestone and a stated credential (or explicit statement that none is issued) on completion. Curriculum development is completed or finalised in the mobilisation phase and refreshed each cohort against employer input, and the design documents the monthly timeline through first-cohort placement, training duration, cohort size and stipend level.

Addresses: R005, R006, R007, R009, R014, R025, R027, R029, R031, R010, R021.

- The anticipated contract start date is January 1, 2026, which sets the mobilisation window before the first cohort. (V. SCHEDULE OF DATES, p. 7 [S18.1])

#### C02. Outreach, recruitment and application/assessment process

Runs a targeted recruitment campaign for New Yorkers underrepresented in tech who cannot reasonably access training on their own, with channel plans for CUNY/SUNY campuses, community-based organisations and workforce partners. Delivers an online application and evaluation process with a documented low-income eligibility determination, a needs assessment that records each participant's barriers to success, and an individual success plan that routes supports accordingly. Recruitment targets are set to meet or exceed the 250-participant floor with a stretch plan above 400, and to keep low-income enrolment above 50%.

Addresses: R008, R011, R012, R013, R038, R040, R092.

#### C03. Training delivery, stipends and wrap-around supports

Delivers and manages the cohort-based training itself: instruction, cohort management, attendance and progress tracking, and professional development support throughout training. Each participant receives a stated stipend at the proposed level plus wrap-around supports — a mentor and a career coach with industry expertise, plus barrier-specific supports identified in the needs assessment. Delivery is staffed for both part-time and full-time schedules so participants with employment or caregiving obligations can complete the program.

Addresses: R015, R016, R030, R051, R084, R027.

#### C04. Employer partnerships and placement engine

Cultivates and maintains relationships with leading New York tech employers to inform curriculum content and generate paid internship and full-time AI openings, supported by employer commitment letters submitted with the proposal. Runs a placement function that matches participants to paid internships (Track A) and full-time AI roles (Track B), prepares them for interviews, and tracks placements through to hire. Placement support is designed to meet the commitment that the majority of graduates secure paid internships or full-time employment in AI or a closely related field.

Addresses: R017, R018, R032, R041, R022, R021, R010.

#### C05. Performance data, reporting and ESD access

Implements a participant data system that captures every required data point — applicant, enrolment and completion counts with low-income percentages, paid internship and full-time placement rates with low-income percentages, participating colleges and employer partners — and makes the data available to ESD on request. ### K01. Schedule A terms unseen but binding, and its concerns must be filed blind

Schedule A sets the standard contract terms the successful respondent must accept, yet its text is not in the RFP. The bidder must decide whether to accept unknown terms and file written concerns with its Administrative Proposal, while the RFP simultaneously forbids including Schedule A information in the submission. If Schedule A contains unacceptable liability, indemnity or data-ownership terms, the bidder either walks away after award or absorbs unplanned legal and insurance cost.

**Why:** Schedule A is referenced as a mandatory contract term (R115, R260) but is absent from the RFP, and R258/R259 pull in opposite directions on how concerns are filed, so the bidder cannot price or accept the terms knowingly. (ii. Administrative Proposal, p. 11 [S28.1]; xi. Schedule A, p. 26 [S57.1])

**Mitigation:** Request Schedule A through the designated contacts before the August 29, 2025 question deadline, and state in the proposal that acceptance is conditional on review of Schedule A; file concerns as a separate letter accompanying the Administrative Proposal, as the proposed solution assumes.

**Related requirements:** R115, R258, R259, R260.

### K02. MWBE 30% and SDVOB 6% goals carry liquidated damages and payment withholding

The contract sets a 30% MWBE and 6% SDVOB participation goal, with good faith effort documentation, quarterly Workforce Utilization Reports and monthly SDVOB compliance reporting. Failure to meet the goals can trigger liquidated damages equal to the shortfall and withholding of payment, and the RFP does not settle whether the goals apply to total contract value or subcontracting spend or whether good faith efforts alone suffice.

**Why:** The damages and withholding provisions are explicit (R191, R195, R196, R250), but the base against which the goals are measured and the waiver standard are left open, so exposure depends on facts the RFP does not settle. (Business Participation Opportunities for MWBEs, p. 19 [S45.1]; Business Participation Opportunities for MWBEs, pp. 19-20 [S45.2]; Equal Employment Opportunity Requirements, pp. 20-21 [S46.1]; Contract Goals, p. 22 [S49.1]; SDVOB Utilization Plan, pp. 22-23 [S50.1]; SDVOB Utilization Plan, p. 23 [S50.2]; Monthly SDVOB Contractor Compliance Report, p. 25 [S53.1])

**Mitigation:** Identify certified MWBE and SDVOB subcontractors before submission and build them into the Utilization Plans and the budget; ask ESD to confirm the measurement base and the waiver route, and price a compliance analyst to run the reporting cadence.

**Related requirements:** R189, R190, R191, R195, R196, R197, R210, R212, R221, R231, R232, R247, R250.

### K03. Stipend level and its share of the $2M per-track cap are undefined

The RFP requires a stipend line in the budget and a stated stipend level in the Project Plan but gives no rate, duration or expected share of the award. If ESD expects a stipend near a full-time wage for 250+ participants, the stipend alone could consume most of the $2M per track, leaving too little for instruction, coaching and placement — and the bidder only learns this after award.

**Why:** Stipends are mandatory (R016, R051, R084) and the cap is fixed (R034), but the RFP leaves the rate and allocation open, so the budget model turns on an unsettled fact. (For Track B: AI Prep 4 Jobs, p. 5 [S9.1]; Grant Funding and Project Timeline, p. 6 [S14.1]; Eligible Expenses, p. 6 [S15.1]; C. Project Plan (30 Points), pp. 8-9 [S22.1])

**Mitigation:** Ask ESD before the deadline what stipend range and share of the award it expects; state the proposed stipend level and the participant count it supports as an explicit assumption in the proposal, and show the cost model at that level.

**Related requirements:** R016, R034, R051, R084.

### K04. Placement commitment above 50% rests on employer letters the bidder must secure

The RFP requires the majority of graduates to secure paid internships or full-time AI roles and requires employer commitment letters with the proposal, but does not define the measurement window or verification method. **Why:** Placement is a core scored outcome (R041, R098) and the proposed solution's six-month measurement window is an inference, not an RFP term, so the commitment could exceed what the bidder can control.
