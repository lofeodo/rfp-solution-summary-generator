## Problem statement

### Client situation

- The RFP is issued by Empire State Development (ESD), a New York State economic development corporation whose mission is to promote a growing state economy, encourage business investment and job creation, and support diverse local economies through loans, grants, tax credits, real estate development and marketing. (Issued: August 8, 2025, p. 1 [S2.1]; I. INTRODUCTION, p. 3 [S5.1])
- ESD's Office of Strategic Workforce Development (OSWD) was created under Governor Hochul's April 2022 $350 million workforce development investment and is charged with aligning workforce efforts to employer needs and creating economic opportunity for unemployed, underemployed and underrepresented workers. (II. OVERVIEW, p. 3 [S6.1])
- ESD identifies Artificial Intelligence as one of the state's highest-growth sectors, citing Cornell Tech research, companies using AI for analytics, automation and decision-making, and the state-backed Empire AI initiative. (II. OVERVIEW, p. 3 [S6.1])
- ESD's stated pain point is that rapid AI advancement risks widening economic inequality because many well-paying AI jobs require specialized education and technical training that lower-income New Yorkers cannot access. (II. OVERVIEW, p. 3 [S6.1])
- ESD also points to tech recruiting practices that favor students at elite schools and those with personal connections to prominent technology companies as a barrier AI Prep is meant to level. (Artificial Intelligence Prep – "AI Prep", pp. 3-4 [S7.1])
- ESD currently has no AI Prep program in place: OSWD is launching AI Prep as a new applied training program and seeks to award contracts to up to two qualified program administrators to develop the curriculum and administer it. (Artificial Intelligence Prep – "AI Prep", pp. 3-4 [S7.1])
- The procurement is subject to a restricted period under the Procurement Lobbying Law running until contract approval, during which respondents may contact only the designated contacts Julia Borukhov and Jordan Kaplan at AI.Prep@esd.ny.gov. (Issued: August 8, 2025, p. 1 [S2.1])

### Goal

- ESD wants up to two program administrators to design the curriculum for and deliver AI Prep across two tracks: Track A "AI Prep 4 Internships" for low-income matriculating undergraduates and Track B "AI Prep 4 Jobs" for low-income New Yorkers with barriers to employment. (Artificial Intelligence Prep – "AI Prep", pp. 3-4 [S7.1])
- Track A requires a comprehensive AI technology training program for low-income college students that includes paid internship placements and builds a talent pipeline for AI employers. (For Track A: AI Prep 4 Internships, p. 4 [S8.1])
- Track B requires an industry-informed AI training program, delivered as an accelerated course or bootcamp as an alternative to traditional degree programs, that connects low-income New Yorkers with barriers to employment to full-time AI jobs. (Artificial Intelligence Prep – "AI Prep", pp. 3-4 [S7.1]; For Track B: AI Prep 4 Jobs, p. 5 [S9.1])
- Deliverables common to both tracks include targeted outreach and recruitment, an application and assessment process, online AI courses built with industry and researcher input, participant stipends and wrap-around supports such as mentors or success coaches, and employer relationship building to generate placements. (For Track A: AI Prep 4 Internships, p. 4 [S8.1]; For Track B: AI Prep 4 Jobs, p. 5 [S9.1])
- The contract runs for an initial term of up to 18 months, with possible renewal or extension based on performance, need and availability of funds, and the anticipated contract start date is January 1, 2026. (For Track B: AI Prep 4 Jobs, p. 5 [S9.1]; V. SCHEDULE OF DATES, p. 7 [S18.1])
- ESD will invest up to $2M per track, up to $4M total, to train and provide stipends for participants. (For Track B: AI Prep 4 Jobs, p. 5 [S9.1])
- Awarded administrators must track and report data including applicant, enrollment and completion counts with low-income percentages, paid internship and full-time placement rates, participating colleges and employer partners, with the exact list set during contract negotiation. (Data Collection, p. 7 [S17.1])

## Proposed solution

Empire State Development (ESD) receives a single, integrated AI Prep program built and delivered by a lead administrator that designs the curriculum, recruits and supports participants, and places them into paid internships (Track A) and full-time AI jobs (Track B). The solution is a build-and-operate engagement: curriculum and assessment assets are developed in a mobilisation phase, then cohorts are recruited, trained online, coached and placed across the 18-month initial term beginning January 1, 2026, with a data and reporting spine running throughout. Delivery is organised in four phases — mobilise and finalise design, recruit and launch the first cohort, deliver and place at scale, and sustain and report — each with monthly milestones and performance estimates against ESD's three metrics. The team is a lead administrator with a program director, curriculum and instructional design staff, employer partnership leads, success coaches and a compliance/reporting function, supported by named subcontractors for outreach and employer engagement. Pricing is built bottom-up within the $2M per track / $4M total cap, with stipends and wraparound supports as explicit budget lines and no indirect costs. Governance, MWBE/SDVOB participation, insurance and contract compliance are handled by a dedicated compliance workstream that runs from proposal submission through quarterly and monthly reporting.

Covers 103 of 103 mandatory requirements.

### Components

#### C01. Program design, curriculum and assessment build

We design an industry-informed AI curriculum built with employer and researcher input, delivered as online courses with defined technical skills and learning milestones (R014, R025). The build covers the full AI technology training program for low-income college students including the internship component (R009), the accelerated bootcamp alternative to a degree for Track B (R007), and the immersive schedule that can run part-time or full-time (R027). We define the credentials awarded on completion (R031) and embed skill-attainment evaluation metrics at each milestone (R029). The curriculum is developed in the mobilisation phase and refreshed each cohort against employer feedback, and the whole delivery plan is scoped to the 18-month initial term with any renewal or extension treated as a separately priced option (R035).

Addresses: R005, R007, R009, R014, R025, R027, R029, R031, R035.

- ESD has no AI Prep program today; OSWD is launching it as a new applied training program and will award contracts to up to two program administrators to develop the curriculum and administer it. (Artificial Intelligence Prep – "AI Prep", pp. 3-4 [S7.1])
- The anticipated contract start date is January 1, 2026, with an initial term of up to 18 months and possible renewal or extension based on performance, need and availability of funds. (V. SCHEDULE OF DATES, p. 7 [S18.1]; For Track B: AI Prep 4 Jobs, p. 5 [S9.1])

#### C02. Outreach, recruitment and eligibility assessment

We run a targeted outreach and recruitment strategy for low-income students and for unemployed, underemployed and underrepresented New Yorkers (R011, R024), reaching New Yorkers underrepresented in tech who cannot reasonably access training on their own (R008). A structured application and evaluation process screens and selects participants (R012), and a needs assessment at intake identifies and addresses each participant's barriers to success (R013). Track A recruitment targets low-income matriculating undergraduates (R006); Track B recruitment targets low-income New Yorkers with barriers to employment (R007).

Addresses: R006, R007, R008, R011, R012, R013, R024.

- ESD's stated pain point is that many well-paying AI jobs require specialised education that lower-income New Yorkers cannot access, and that recruiting favours elite-school students and personal connections. (II. OVERVIEW, p. 3 [S6.1]; Artificial Intelligence Prep – "AI Prep", pp. 3-4 [S7.1])

#### C03. Training delivery, coaching and participant supports

### K01. Program scale and stipend level undefined against a hard $2M per track cap

The RFP caps funding at $2M per track but never states a target participant count, cohort size, stipend amount or stipend duration, while also requiring a 250-participant minimum, priority above 400, and a majority-placement outcome. The proposed solution assumes roughly 400 participants per track in cohorts of 50 with a stipend rate that leaves most of the award for delivery — an assumption, not a settled fact. If ESD expects a higher stipend or a larger cohort, the unit economics break and the bid is either unaffordable or under-scoped.

**Why:** The cap is firm and disqualifying (budgets above $2M per track are not considered) while the participant count and stipend level that drive cost are absent from the RFP; the solution's 400-per-track sizing is explicitly an inference. (For Track B: AI Prep 4 Jobs, p. 5 [S9.1]; Program Goals and Objectives, pp. 5-6 [S12.1]; Grant Funding and Project Timeline, p. 6 [S14.1]; Eligible Expenses, p. 6 [S15.1])

**Mitigation:** Submit a written question before the August 29, 2025 deadline asking for the expected participant count per track and the expected stipend level and duration; state in the proposal the participant count, cohort size and stipend rate the price is built on, and price a lower-stipend/higher-volume variant as an option.

**Related requirements:** R034, R038, R040, R051, R090.

### K02. Schedule A, Appendix A, Appendix B and Schedule B are not in the RFP

Four referenced documents — Schedule A (standard contract terms), Appendix A (ESD IT Security Standard Brief), Appendix B (Diversity Practices Questionnaire) and Schedule B (MWBE requirements and procedures) — are cited but not included in the RFP text. **Why:** The documents are named in the RFP but absent from it, and two of them (Appendix B, Schedule A) are mandatory submission or contract-acceptance items; the solution simply assumes they will be obtained and applied as issued. (APPENDICES, p. 2 [S4.1]; E. Diversity Practices (10 pts), p. 10 [S24.1]; ii. Administrative Proposal, p. 11 [S28.1]; Equal Employment Opportunity Requirements, pp. 20-21 [S46.1]; xi. ### K11. Staff approval and continuity obligations constrain a delivery team built on named specialists

Contractor staff are subject to ESD approval, staff continuity through completion is expected, and any substitute must be presented with an experience summary and interviewed before approval. The solution commits to continuity and to naming staff in the price proposal. If a key curriculum or employer-partnership lead leaves, the replacement cycle could stall delivery and the priced staffing plan becomes inaccurate.

**Why:** The approval and interview steps add delay to any replacement, and the solution's price is built on named roles with defined percentages of time. (Contractor Staff, p. 14 [S35.1]; D. Cost of Services (20 Points), p. 9 [S23.1])

**Mitigation:** Name a deputy for each key role in the proposal and state that substitutes will be pre-identified with experience summaries ready; ask ESD how quickly approval decisions will be made.

**Related requirements:** R139, R140, R141, R142, R092.

### K12. Insurance and compliance obligations may exceed a training provider's current cover

The selected respondent must carry $1M/$2M commercial general liability, $1M sexual abuse and molestation cover, commercial auto where a vehicle is used, and statutory workers' compensation, employer's liability and disability cover, with ESD as additional insured and a waiver of subrogation. The RFP qualifies the list as applying 'at a minimum and to the extent applicable' without saying which coverages attach to an AI training engagement. Sexual abuse and molestation cover in particular may not be in a training provider's existing programme and takes time to bind.

**Why:** The coverages are mandatory contract conditions but the RFP does not confirm which apply to this scope, and the solution simply asserts it carries all of them. (xiii. Insurance Requirements, p. 26 [S59.1])

**Mitigation:** Ask ESD which coverages are mandatory for this scope and whether the waiver of subrogation is required on all policies; obtain broker quotes for sexual abuse and molestation cover before submission and carry the premium in the budget.

**Related requirements:** R251, R252, R253, R254, R255, R256, R257.
